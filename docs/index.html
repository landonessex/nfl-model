Power ratings

Points better than a league-average team on a neutral field, adjusted for who each team has actually played. A matchup line is roughly the difference between two of these, plus home field.

How it works

Ratings are solved, not averaged

Dividing points scored by the league average is readable and it's wrong by Week 6, because a team that drew the four worst defences in football looks like a juggernaut. Attack and defense here are solved against each other, iterating until they settle, so a rating means what a team would do against an average opponent — the only version that transfers to next week.

Two estimates, blended

The ratings give one expected score. A ridge regression on four box-score statistics — passing yards, rushing yards, takeaways, giveaways — gives a second, built from different information. They blend 55/45. When they disagree sharply, that game is worth looking at by hand rather than betting.

The market gets a vote early

In Week 1 there is no 2026 data and the market has every roster move priced. The model starts at 75% market weight and decays toward 25% as its own sample builds. This feels like cheating. It is the single most valuable rule in the file.

Everything runs in your browser

GitHub Pages serves static files and nothing else, so there is no server doing the maths. The ratings are fitted by a scheduled job and committed as JSON; the twenty thousand simulations behind every number on this page run locally when you change a dropdown. Nothing you type is sent anywhere.

For research and entertainment. No model reliably beats a sharp closing line. Bet only what you can afford to lose, if at all.
